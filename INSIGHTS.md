# What the numbers say

At 30 June 2025 Australia had 228,259 operational residential aged care places. That is 66.1 places for every 1,000 people aged 70 or over, using the ABS estimated resident population. Holding the long-standing planning benchmark of 78 would take another 40,939 places today. The ratio was 81.7 in 2015, and the place gap against that benchmark has grown by 49,854 places. Last year the stocktake shows a net change of 794 operational places. States have said about 10,600 new places a year are needed.

## National supply

- Operational residential places, 30 June 2025: **228,259**.
- ABS estimated resident population aged 70+: **3,451,258**. Aged 80+: **1,247,910**.
- Places per 1,000 people aged 70+: **66.1** (2015: 81.7).
- Places per 1,000 people aged 80+: **182.9**.
- Department of Health published ratio, 30 June 2025: **67.0** per 1,000 aged 70+. That ratio uses a customised population projection based on the 2017 ERP, so it is not the same denominator as the ABS estimate above.
- Places short of the 78-per-1,000 benchmark, on the ABS population: **40,939**.
- Net change in operational residential places, 2024 to 2025: **794**.

## Where the gap is largest

The planning region with the most places still needed to reach 78 per 1,000, using the Department's published ratio and the population implied by it, is **Southern Metro** (VIC): **1,853** places, ratio **69.0**.

State gaps against 78 per 1,000 people aged 70+ at 30 June 2025, ABS population:

- New South Wales: 67.4 per 1,000 aged 70+, 74,359 places, shortfall 11,736.
- Queensland: 63.3 per 1,000 aged 70+, 44,361 places, shortfall 10,292.
- Western Australia: 59.6 per 1,000 aged 70+, 20,845 places, shortfall 6,441.
- Victoria: 70.9 per 1,000 aged 70+, 60,800 places, shortfall 6,077.
- South Australia: 68.1 per 1,000 aged 70+, 19,175 places, shortfall 2,800.
- Tasmania: 56.9 per 1,000 aged 70+, 5,239 places, shortfall 1,940.
- Australian Capital Territory: 56.1 per 1,000 aged 70+, 2,752 places, shortfall 1,071.
- Northern Territory: 45.4 per 1,000 aged 70+, 728 places, shortfall 522.

## Pace versus the reported need

ABC News on 9 November 2026 quoted SA Health Minister Blair Boyer: at least 10,600 additional federal residential places are needed nationally each year over the next two decades, and last year just 800 beds were added. A federal spokesperson told the ABC the government is supporting construction of around 5,000 additional beds a year. Those are reported claims.

On the stocktake, operational residential places changed by **794** between 30 June 2024 and 30 June 2025.

Using the ABS medium projection, keeping today's national ratio of 66.1 through to 2031 would require about **51,585** more places in total if none of today's places close, or about **8,598** a year for six years. Reaching 78 per 1,000 by 2031 would require about **101,776** more places, about **16,963** a year.

## Service list, 2021 to 2025

The annual service lists are the operational registry used for the change-data feed. They are matched on state, care type, service name and suburb because the national workbook does not include a stable service number. Spelling changes (for example a town name) look like a closure plus an opening, so those counts are an upper bound.

Residential, Multi-Purpose and National Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Flexible services:

- 2021 to 2022: 92 services appeared, 127 disappeared, 121 changed their residential place count, 2,799 had some operational field change.
- 2022 to 2023: 160 services appeared, 186 disappeared, 130 changed their residential place count, 2,705 had some operational field change.
- 2023 to 2024: 210 services appeared, 230 disappeared, 115 changed their residential place count, 164 had some operational field change.
- 2024 to 2025: 303 services appeared, 328 disappeared, 162 changed their residential place count, 302 had some operational field change.

Listed residential places on the 30 June 2025 service list sum to **228,259**, across **5,378** services. The stocktake's operational residential total is **228,259**. Those two totals meet. The confidentialised GEN CURF sums to **224,493** places on rows whose program is residential care. It is lower because the stocktake also counts flexible residential places delivered through Multi-Purpose Services and the National Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Flexible Aged Care Program, which the CURF stores under those program names.

## Assumptions that move the 2031 numbers

- The projection is ABS series 29, the medium series (series B), not a forecast.
- State 70+ growth is applied to the 2025 ABS ERP. The projection's own 2025 level is used only to measure the growth rate between 2025 and 2031.
- Each Aged Care Planning Region keeps its June 2025 share of its state's 70+ population. Regions that age faster or slower than their state are not separately observed.
- The 80+ ratio for a region applies the state's 80+ share of the 70+ population.
- 'Extra places' assumes the 2025 operational stock stays open. Closures would add to the gap. Places already in the pipeline are not subtracted, because the stocktake's provisional places are not modelled as certain openings.
- 78 per 1,000 is a planning benchmark, not a count of people who need a bed. Some of the people behind the South Australian hospital figure need a place now; the ratio gap is a population-capacity comparison.

## What this does not measure

Operational places are not occupied beds, vacancies, or staffed beds. A region can have places on the stocktake and still have no bed free this week. Hospital patients waiting for a place are taken from ABC reports of state figures, not from this dataset. Home care packages are mostly assigned to people rather than services, so they are outside the residential place counts.
